# A tribute to WarGames

JOSHUA Terminal is my personal tribute to *WarGames* (1983). The name JOSHUA, and the idea of a strategic supercomputer called WOPR that has to learn the difference between a game and a catastrophe, come straight from that film — as inspiration, not as a claim to the story itself.

*WarGames* left a mark on a whole generation of hackers, in the original and best sense of that word: people curious enough to explore how systems work, and patient enough to actually understand them. For a lot of people who grew up with modems, acoustic couplers, and terminals as objects that were still mysterious and barely within reach, the film put those things on screen and made them feel like something you might one day get to touch yourself. I was one of those people. The year before I saw the film, a visionary middle-school teacher had introduced me to a ZX80 in an afternoon course he had organized at school. That first encounter, together with the acoustic coupler I saw in WarGames, stayed with me: they became two of the strongest influences that, years later, led me to embrace computing and the Internet.

This project tries to recreate a small piece of that feeling in a browser tab: a green prompt, a machine that seems to be thinking, a game that turns into something bigger than a game.

The message underneath all of it is the same one the film carried: some conflicts have no winning move, and the only sane response to that realization is not to play them out for real.

Thank you to the writers, director, cast, and everyone else who made *WarGames* — for a story that still holds up, and for putting that particular idea in front of an audience in a way a lot of people never forgot.

This project is an independent fan tribute and is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or produced in association with the makers or rights holders of *WarGames*.
